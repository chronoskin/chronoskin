Today's featured article

A water clock measures time by the regulated flow of liquid into or out of a vessel. Together with the sundial it is among the oldest timekeeping instruments, and it stayed in use where the sky was overcast or the hour fell at night. Outflow clocks are marked on the inside of a draining bowl; inflow clocks read a float that rises in a second vessel fed at a steady rate.

Later builders added gear trains, bells and moving figures, and a constant-level tank to keep the pressure even. The instrument was only displaced by the verge escapement and, later, the pendulum. (more...)

For the sinking-bowl timer used to share out irrigation turns, see Water clock (irrigation).

A water clock is an instrument that measures time by the regulated flow of liquid into or out of a vessel, where the amount is then read against a scale. With the sundial it is among the oldest instruments for telling the hour, and unlike the sundial it works indoors, under cloud and at night.[1]

The simplest form is a bowl with a small hole near its base. Makers soon found that the flow slows as the level falls, and much of the later history of the instrument is the search for an even rate.[2]

[edit]Principle

[edit]Outflow clocks

An outflow clock is a vessel that drains through a narrow opening. Hours are marked on its inner wall, and the wall is often sloped so that equal drops in level stand for nearly equal times. A formula of the form t = k * sqrt(h) describes the emptying of a straight-sided vessel.

A reconstructed clock tower with its feed tank on the upper floor

[edit]Inflow clocks

An inflow clock collects the water in a second, straight-sided vessel in which a float carries a pointer. Because the receiving vessel does not affect the rate, the scale can be evenly divided, provided the supply is steady. The usual remedies were:

  • an overflow tank that keeps the head of water constant;
  • a conical float valve that closes the inlet as the level rises;
  • a set of interchangeable scales, one for each month, where hours varied with the season.
