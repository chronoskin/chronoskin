Tables were fine

Jonas Pell, issue 26, October 2016. 2,400 words and 31 letters.

Send a letter

The essay

Nobody misses the spacer image

First printed 2016-10-04, corrected once.

But the grid of numbers that a table gives you for nothing is still the clearest thing a browser can draw, and the first essay in this issue counts how often we rebuild it badly.

What a table already does

  • Lines its columns up without being asked.
  • Reads aloud in the right order.
  • Prints.

Try it

Open any page of figures and run view-source on it. If the numbers are not in a table, ask what was gained.

Method

How a page with nothing on it gets written, read and printed once a month.

Write to us

How an issue is made

Six people who have never met send each other text files. On the last Friday of the month the files that everyone has read become an issue, and the rest wait.

The order of work

  1. An idea, in one sentence, to all six.
  2. A draft under three thousand words.
  3. Two readers, neither of them the writer.
  4. Proof, then print, then leave it alone.

What print means here

An essay is printed when its address will never change. After that only a correction is allowed, dated, at the foot.

House rules

Text only. No picture unless the essay is about that picture. Every link goes where it says. Nothing moves.
May an essay carry code?

Yes, as text, in the fixed face. Never as a picture of code.

Who decides what is printed?

All six. One refusal sends a draft back to its writer with the reason.

Can a printed essay change?

Only by a dated correction at its foot.
