About the place

Four square kilometres of reed and cut drain between the sluice and the railway. There is one path, two bridges and no shelter, which is why most of these were made in a hurry.

The best hour is the one before the pump at Cold Harbour starts. After that everything on the fen has a hum under it, and you can find it in number 236 if you listen on the right.

Next place along the drain

No recordings from “Fenny Lock”.

Nobody has walked there with a recorder yet.

Notes by the maker

The fen is loudest when nothing happens

This one was made from the second drain bridge with the recorder on the rail, and the knocking at four minutes is the loose plank of number 218, not a fault in the file.

  • The pump at Cold Harbour, far off, on the right.
  • One heron, once, at 11 min 20 s.

About this catalogue

Who makes it, how a recording gets a number and what you may do with one.

How a recording gets a number

It gets the next one, on the day it is made, whether it is good or not. Numbers are never reused: a file that is withdrawn leaves a gap, and the gap stays in the index.

  1. Walk. Record. Write the place and the time on the back of a hand.
  2. Cut nothing but the walk to and from the spot.
  3. Give it a number, a sentence and a length.

What you may do with one

Anything. Play it, cut it up, sell what you make from it. Nobody needs to be asked and nobody needs to be named.

What is not here

Pictures, maps, players, accounts. A file opens in whatever your machine already has.

What it is recorded with

Whatever fits in a coat pocket and survives being sat on. The list changes when something breaks, and every change is written up in the notes.

  • A hand recorder, bought used in 2014, dropped twice.
  • Two small microphones on a wire coat hanger.
  • A hydrophone made from a piezo disc and a jam jar lid.

Who keeps this

Tove Lindqvist walks the same nine kilometres most weeks with a recorder in a coat pocket, and answers mail slowly.
