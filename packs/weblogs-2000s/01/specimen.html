14 March 2005

The drying rack is finished

After three weekends of sanding, the rack finally holds forty sheets without sagging. I built it from offcuts that the timber yard on Fennick Row was about to throw away, and it cost less than a tin of ink.

The first run on it was a set of two-colour postcards. Registration drifted by about a millimetre, which nobody but me will notice.

What I would change

The shelves are a little too close together. If you build one, leave room for your hand.

  • Space the dowels at 35mm rather than 25mm.
  • Wax the runners before the first use.
  • Label the shelves, or you will lose track of the order.

A note on the counter script

The sheet counter on this page is a few lines of shell. Run count-sheets --week and it prints the total; the source is in the projects section.

Ink that will not dry in February

9 March 2005, filed under Workshop. Three comments so far.

All entries

Cold rooms and oil-based ink do not get along. The cards I printed on the first of the month were still tacky on the eighth, and the shed has not been above nine degrees since Christmas.

Ink dries by taking up air, not by losing water. Warm the sheet, not the tin.

Three things worked: a drop of drier in the ink, a fan heater under the rack for the first hour, and thinner paper. Laying the sheets on the press blanket to warm them did not; the blanket now has forty faint postcards on it.

About the author

Mara Ellison prints cards and repairs old plotters in a shed behind her house. She answers mail slowly and comments quickly. 12 entries this month, filed under workshop machines. Comments on an entry are open for thirty days, closing in the last week and closed after that.

What this is

chronoskin began in 2002 as a list of things that went wrong on the press. It still is that, with three years of archives and the occasional thing that went right.

What it is not

A shop, a tutorial or a place to ask what your old press is worth. I do not know what your old press is worth.
