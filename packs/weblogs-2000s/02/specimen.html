Thursday, 9 June 2005

A county map drawn from bus timetables

Somebody in the hill villages has redrawn the whole county using nothing but the printed bus timetables: places sit as far apart as the ride takes, not as far as the road runs. The coast comes out twice as long as the atlas has it.

Distances are in minutes. Where no bus goes, there is no land.

Why it works

The valleys with one bus a day stretch out like taffy, and the market town shrinks to a dot.

  • Printed on one sheet, folded in eight.
  • Sunday services are drawn as dotted lines.
  • The legend is longer than the map.

Make your own

The author’s script reads a timetable as plain text: busmap --minutes routes.txt writes the outline.

Kite made from one newspaper and no glue

Wednesday, 8 June 2005, by Tobias Renn. Three comments.

Front page

A retired printer has worked out how to fold a full broadsheet into a kite that needs no glue, no tape and no sticks. The spine is the paper itself, rolled tight along the fold.

Use yesterday’s paper. Today’s is still too damp to hold a crease.

I built one at lunch and it flew for nine minutes over the allotments before the sports pages gave way.

Who writes this

Four friends who met on a kite field and never stopped sending each other links. 38 entries this week, mostly under maps radio. A suggested link is posted, waiting or declined, and we say which.

Our linking policy

We link to things one person made and to pages that will still be there next year. We do not link to shops, and we never take anything for a link.

Corrections

When we get something wrong we strike it through and say so underneath. Nothing is quietly removed; the archives keep everything.

Write to us

Links go through the suggestion form. For anything else, a postcard to the kite field finds all four of us.
