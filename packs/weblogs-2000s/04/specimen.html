May 17

Forty-one moths and one very cross wren

The warm spell finally filled the trap. I opened the lid at five to find three lime hawk moths asleep on the egg boxes and a wren on the fence telling me exactly what she thought of the whole business.

Everything was counted, photographed on the same grey card and let go into the hedge before the sun reached it.

If the lamp is on, somebody is counting. If it is off, somebody is asleep.

What changed since April

Twice the species and three times the numbers, which is what a week of mild nights will do.

  • First hawk moths of the year, eleven days earlier than last spring.
  • No frost damage on the box lid this time.
  • The red torch is still the best thing I own.

Keeping the tally

The counts live in one text file. Running tally --month may prints the table that goes into the field lists.

The first warm night

22 April 2006, filed under Moth trap. Three comments so far.

The whole list

Apr 22

Eleven degrees at midnight, no wind, and cloud like a lid. I lit the lamp at nine and by ten the sheet behind it was moving.

The first warm night is not in the calendar. You find it by leaving the lamp on.

Fifty-three moths of fourteen kinds by first light, which is more than the whole of March. The owls took no notice at all.

About, and how to write

Who keeps this journal, and where a note should go.

Read the journal

Who keeps this

Idris Vane teaches woodwork by day and counts moths before breakfast. 9 entries this month, mostly under moth trap owls. Comments are open for four weeks, closing in the last few days and closed after that.

What gets written down

Whatever came to the lamp, what the owls left under the tower, and the sky when there was one. The counts end up in the field lists.

What does not

Where the owls roost, and anything that would bring a crowd to a small garden at midnight.

Write to me

Answered before breakfast, most days.
