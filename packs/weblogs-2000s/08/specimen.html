The out-apiary by Wilma Aksnes 21:12

WAWilma

First flight of the year

Eleven degrees at noon and every hive was flying. Hive four, which I had written off in January, was the busiest of the lot, with pollen coming in on two legs out of three.

I lifted each box from behind to judge the stores. Two are light and will get fondant this week; the rest can wait for the willow.

Bees on the roof by Teodor Lind 18:47

TLTeodor

The landlord has questions

Six floors up there is nobody to sting, I said. He wanted to know where forty thousand of anything go when it rains.

Inside, mostly. They are better at staying dry than I am.

We agreed on one more season and a jar for every flat.

How this works

One page, one script and forty-eight people who write about bees.

Add your weblog

A page that reads the others for you

Nobody writes here. Every half hour a script asks each weblog on the list for its feed, sorts what is new by time and writes the front page again. The entries stay where they were written; this is only a window onto them.

What you can do with it

  • Read the river in your browser, newest first.
  • Follow one combined feed instead of forty-eight.
  • Take the whole list away as one file and read it your own way.

For keepers of other planets

The list lives in one plain file. Each weblog is a section headed by its feed address, with a name line and an optional face line; set items_per_page = 40 to change how far back the river runs.
