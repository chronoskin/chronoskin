Keeping a timeline straight across four narrators

Started by tessellate on 11 Jun 2005. 63 replies, 2,417 views. New

Post reply
Posted 11 Jun 2005 09:14Post 1 of 64
tessellate Workshop host Posts: 2,318 Joined: Mar 2003

The novel has four narrators and covers eleven days. Each of them tells some of the same hours, and by the third draft I had two people eating breakfast in different towns on the same morning.

I have tried index cards, a wall chart and a spreadsheet. The cards get shuffled, the chart does not travel, and the spreadsheet tells me day 6, 14:00 without telling me who knows what by then. What do the rest of you actually use?

Draft four. Do not ask.

oldharbour Moderator Posts: 7,540 Joined: Jan 2002
tessellate wrote: The spreadsheet tells me day 6, 14:00 without telling me who knows what by then.

Then the clock is not your problem; knowledge is. Keep the spreadsheet for the hours, and add one column per narrator that holds a single sentence: what this person believes at this moment. Read down a column and you have that narrator's whole arc.

It is tedious for a week. After that you will catch every contradiction before a reader does.

Critique the writing, never the writer.

Late to this, but the one thing that worked for me was drawing the eleven days as a single line on a roll of lining paper and giving each narrator a coloured pencil. Where two colours cross, both of them were in the room.

It fits in a drawer, which a wall chart does not. Number the days in pen before you start.

Help and house rules

How the circle works and what the moderators expect. Last revised 03 Jan 2005.

Ask a moderator

House rules for critique

The circle works because people give as much as they ask for. Critique the writing, never the writer. Everything else follows from that.

Before you post an excerpt

Say what stage the piece is at and what kind of reading you want. A first draft and a final proof need very different eyes.

  • Give two critiques for every one you ask for.
  • Keep excerpts under 2,000 words; link to the rest.
  • Mark quoted passages with [quote] so replies stay readable.

When you give a critique

Start with what the piece is trying to do, then say where it does and does not get there. Point at lines. A critique the writer can act on is worth ten that only say whether you liked it.

Copyright

Whatever you post remains yours. Do not post work that is not.

Moderation

Moderators may move a topic to the right forum or lock one that has run its course. Disagree by private message, not in the topic.
