A status line in forty lines

Every few years I install a prompt framework, admire it for a week and remove it when the shell takes half a second to start. This time I wrote down what I actually read in a prompt. It was three things.

What a prompt has to say

  • Where I am, shortened to the last two directories.
  • Whether the last command failed, as its exit code.
  • The branch, when there is one, and nothing when there is none.

That is one call to pwd, one read of $? and one small file under .git. No process has to start.

The whole of it

lantern() {
  code=$?
  dir=${PWD#"${PWD%/*/*}/"}
  [ $code -ne 0 ] && err=" [$code]" || err=""
  printf '%s%s $ ' "$dir" "$err"
}

The remaining thirty lines read the branch and choose colours. They are in the repository.

I repair and program computers for a small workshop. In the evenings I write the tools I wished I had during the day, and then I write down how they work, because in two years I will have forgotten.

What you will find here

  • Short programs, explained line by line.
  • Repairs of machines that were meant to be thrown away.
  • Opinions about plain text, held loosely.

Everything here may be copied. A link back is kind but not required.
